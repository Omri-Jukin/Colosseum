# Historical Claim Ledger

The live experience uses short museum-label wording rather than trying to
compress every scholarly qualification into the viewport. This ledger records
the source behind each displayed claim and the care taken with its wording.
The source links also appear beside the relevant claim in the interface.

## I — Completion and naming

- **Displayed datum:** `80 CE`
- **Claim:** Titus completed and dedicated the Flavian Amphitheatre in 80 CE.
  The name “Colosseum” developed because the building stood near Nero’s
  colossal statue.
- **Source:** [The Flavian Dynasty (69–96), The Metropolitan Museum of
  Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/the-flavian-dynasty-69-96)
- **Wording note:** “Completed” describes the dedication under Titus; later
  Flavian work, including Domitian’s underground construction, is acknowledged
  separately in stage VI.

## II — Age and continued survival

- **Displayed datum:** `≈ 1,946 years`
- **Claim:** The structure combines travertine, tuff, brick, and mortar and has
  survived through repeated repair and ongoing conservation.
- **Source:** [Conservation of the Colosseum, Parco archeologico del
  Colosseo](https://colosseo.it/en/restauri/colosseum/)
- **Wording note:** 2026 minus 80 gives approximately 1,946 years. The
  experience deliberately says “nearly 1,950 years” and “almost two
  millennia,” not a literal 2,000 years. It also avoids implying that the
  monument survived untouched.

## III — Entrances and crowd routing

- **Displayed datum:** `76`
- **Claim:** Spectators used 76 numbered entrances and followed routes matching
  their admission tesserae.
- **Source:** [The Cavea of the Colosseum and the Belvedere Terrace, Parco
  archeologico del
  Colosseo](https://colosseo.it/en/marvels/the-cavea-of-the-colosseum-and-the-belvedere-terrace/)
- **Wording note:** The remaining four of the original 80 ground-level arches
  were principal entrances rather than ordinary numbered public entrances.

## IV — Seating and social hierarchy

- **Displayed datum:** `SOCIAL ORDER`
- **Claim:** Senators occupied the lowest seats, equites sat behind them, and
  lower-status groups were seated progressively higher.
- **Source:** [The Cavea of the Colosseum and the Belvedere Terrace, Parco
  archeologico del
  Colosseo](https://colosseo.it/en/marvels/the-cavea-of-the-colosseum-and-the-belvedere-terrace/)

## V — Types of spectacle

- **Displayed datum:** `MORE THAN DUELS`
- **Claim:** The amphitheatre hosted both gladiatorial contests and animal
  hunts.
- **Source:** [The Colosseum, Parco archeologico del
  Colosseo](https://colosseo.it/en/area/the-colosseum/)
- **Wording note:** The interface does not invent a single “typical” program or
  imply that every event used the same format.

## VI — The underground system

- **Displayed datum:** `14 CORRIDORS`
- **Claim:** The underground area used trapdoors, freight lifts, winches, and
  pulleys. Early works were timber; Domitian later rebuilt the system in
  masonry. The visible complex is organized into 14 corridors.
- **Source:** [The Underground Levels of the Colosseum, Parco archeologico del
  Colosseo](https://colosseo.it/en/marvels/the-underground-levels-of-the-colosseum/)

## VII — Collapse and the surviving outer ring

- **Displayed datum:** `33 OF 80`
- **Claim:** The earthquake of 847 contributed to damage and later collapse on
  the southern side. Today, 33 of the original 80 external arcades survive on
  the northern side.
- **Source:** [Life in the Colosseum in the Middle Ages: Domus and Cryptae,
  Parco archeologico del
  Colosseo](https://colosseo.it/en/opere/life-in-the-colosseum-in-the-middle-ages-1-domus-and-cryptae/)
- **Wording note:** The interface treats loss as a long history of earthquakes,
  reuse, and material removal rather than attributing the present ruin to one
  event alone.

## VIII — Present-day stewardship

- **Displayed datum:** `3D SURVEY · 2024`
- **Claim:** A complete 3D survey and heritage building information model have
  supported conservation work since 2024.
- **Source:** [Conservation of the Colosseum, Parco archeologico del
  Colosseo](https://colosseo.it/en/restauri/colosseum/)

## Spatial design reference

The project-authored lower route is an interpretive visitor path, not a literal
archaeological reconstruction. Its visual language is informed by the
Colosseum’s annular and radial circulation routes:

- [Southern Ambulatories, Parco archeologico del
  Colosseo](https://colosseo.it/en/southern-ambulatories/)
- [Restoration of the Floor Surfaces in Piazza del Colosseo, Parco
  archeologico del
  Colosseo](https://colosseo.it/en/restauri/colosseum/restoration-of-the-floor-surfaces-in-piazza-del-colosseo/)

The final pavilion sits beyond the ancient outer ring and is intentionally
contemporary so it cannot be mistaken for a reconstruction of an ancient Roman
ticket booth.
